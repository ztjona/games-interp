---

## 1. Project framing

**Question.** Do convolutional DQN agents trained on board games learn
human-interpretable strategic concepts (threats, completable lines,
piece attributes), and can sparse dictionary learning recover those
concepts from internal activations?

**Method.** Train SAEs on activations of hookable layers (`conv1`,
`conv2`, `fc1`, dual Q-heads) of a Quarto agent. Define a battery of
binary "Board State Properties" (BSPs) — cell occupancy, cell
attributes (tall / black / square / with-hole), threat lines, 2×2
threat squares, offered piece, game phase, global features. Match SAE
features to BSPs via per-feature precision / recall / F1, and compare
against a **linear-probe (LP) ceiling** on the same activations.

**Headline metric (adopted 2026-05-11).** Every winner claim reports
three coverage numbers side-by-side:

- `F1` — literature standard,
- `MCC` — base-rate-invariant,
- `F1-lift` — F1 above the trivial `2p/(1+p)` baseline (the
  recommended headline metric; ~3.5× more discriminating than raw F1
  for trained-vs-random comparisons).

All winners are also compared against a **random-network control**
(same architecture, untrained weights) and an LP trained on the same
activations.

### 1.1 Metric definitions

Given a binary BSP with positive base rate `p ∈ (0, 1)` and a candidate
detector (an SAE feature thresholded at zero, or a linear-probe logit)
producing the 2 × 2 contingency table `{TP, FP, FN, TN}`, the three
coverage metrics we report are:

- **F1.** Harmonic mean of precision and recall.

  `F1 = 2·TP / (2·TP + FP + FN)`

  Literature-standard, but **base-rate-confounded**: a degenerate
  "always-positive" detector gets `F1 = 2p / (1 + p)`, which is 0.667
  at `p = 0.5` and so dominates rare-concept rankings if used alone.

- **MCC** (Matthews correlation coefficient). Pearson correlation
  between the binary prediction and the label.

  `MCC = (TP·TN − FP·FN) / √((TP+FP)(TP+FN)(TN+FP)(TN+FN))`

  Symmetric in classes, base-rate-invariant, and collapses to 0 for
  the always-positive detector. We use MCC as the **sanity check** —
  if MCC and F1 disagree about which run is best, F1 is exploiting a
  base-rate artifact.

- **F1-lift.** F1 minus the trivial baseline, clipped at zero.

  `F1_lift = max(0, F1 − 2p / (1 + p))`

  This is the **recommended headline metric** for trained-vs-random and
  trained-vs-trained comparisons. The trivial-baseline correction
  cancels the same artifact MCC handles, but stays on an F1-shaped
  scale (so it composes additively when averaging across BSPs with
  different base rates). Empirically the trained-vs-random gap in
  F1-lift is **~3.5× larger** than in raw F1 (Phase 2A, 2026-05-11) —
  the metric is much more discriminating in practice.

All three are derivable from the same `TP/FP/FN/TN` matrices that the
SAE eval pipeline already computes, so the extra cost is negligible
and `scripts/backfill_eval_metrics.py` retro-fills older registry
entries.

## 2. Where we are now (Phase 2B complete, 2026-05-19)

### 2.1 Champions

to expose different training-time interventions:

|---|---|---|---|

### 2.2 BSP sets (ground-truth concept battery)

Two named BSP sets — **gorilla** (164 raw game properties) and **hawk**
(173 Nanda-style reframed properties) — are alternative bases for the
same underlying threat concepts. We always evaluate them *separately*
because the union double-counts redundant signals.

**Gorilla (164 BSPs) — raw game properties** ("does this strategic
fact hold on the board?"):

| Category | Count | Description |
|---|---:|---|
| `cell_occupancy` | 16 | Is cell (r, c) occupied? (4 × 4 grid) |
| `cell_attribute` | 64 | Binary attribute of piece at (r, c) (16 cells × 4 attrs: tall / black / square / with-hole) |
| `threat_line` | 40 | 10 lines × 4 attrs — 3-of-4 pieces in line share attribute *and* 1 cell empty |
| `threat_square_2x2` | 36 | 9 squares × 4 attrs — same conjunction over a 2×2 sub-grid |
| `offered_piece` | 4 | Binary attribute of currently offered piece |
| `game_phase` | 3 | early (0–5 pieces) / mid (6–11) / late (12–16) |
| `global` | 1 | `winning_move_exists` — can the offered piece be placed to win? |

**Hawk (173 BSPs) — reframed properties** (Nanda-inspired alternative
basis; OthelloGPT was non-linear under BLACK/WHITE but linear under
MINE/YOURS, so we test whether Quarto threats are non-linear under
"3-of-4 + empty" but linear under count-based / completability
reframings):

| Category | Count | Description |
|---|---:|---|
| `reframed_count` | 40 | ≥3 occupied cells in line share attribute (*superset* of `threat_line`; ignores empty-cell requirement) |
| `reframed_completable` | 40 | `threat_line` *and* offered piece has matching attribute (*strict subset* — gates threat on actionability) |
| `reframed_any_threat` | 10 | OR-reduction across 4 attrs per line |
| `reframed_sq_count` | 36 | Same superset over 2×2 squares |
| `reframed_sq_completable` | 36 | Same subset over 2×2 squares |
| `reframed_sq_any_threat` | 9 | OR-reduction across attrs per square |
| `reframed_global` | 2 | `board_threat_exists` + `board_completable_exists` (line OR square) |

Per-champion variants (`gorillaS4`, `gorillaTa`, …) recompute the same
schema against that champion's self-play position distribution, so
base rates match the activation tensor.

Why the dual basis matters: in Phase 1D the raw `threat_line` LP F1 was
0.022 (essentially noise) but the reframed `reframed_count` LP F1 was
0.315 — a **14× improvement under the same activations**. The threat
information was there; the gorilla basis just couldn't see it. We
report both bases now in every winner claim. Full schema and
gorilla ↔ hawk correspondence in `BSP-schema-summary.md`.

## 4. Provisional findings worth highlighting

(Tagged provisional because the conv2/hawk wall is still being closed —
see §6.)

**Claim 1. champTa is strictly better than champS4** on every behavioral
test *and* every interpretability cell. The pre-registered "oracle
shortcut" failure mode (good play but worse concept content) is
rejected.

**Claim 2. fc1 SAEs beat conv2 SAEs on hawk, even though the conv2 LP
beats the fc1 LP.** Most surprising finding of the sweep. Two
non-exclusive mechanisms predict this:
- *Sparsity allocation under base-rate skew.* At conv2 the 512-d
  activation space contains many high-base-rate cell-attribute signals
  competing for k slots; at fc1 the representation is more pre-mixed,
  so a sparse feature there can land on a "threat exists" axis more
  easily.
- *Optimization rather than representation.* SAE training loss is
  dominated by dense cell-attribute MSE; rare threat signals contribute
  negligibly. LP does not suffer this because each BSP gets its own
  classifier.

Both mechanisms predict the same fix: **concept-targeted / anchored
SAEs**, or significantly **wider expansion** at fixed k (§6).

**Claim 3. The architecture winner transfers.** `E05 BatchTopK k=32
exp=8 s4.conv2` is the gorilla winner on both champions. On hawk, both
winners are at fc1 — the *hook* is shared even though the SAE family
differs (TopK on S4, JumpReLU on Ta).

**Claim 4. The 11 % conv2/hawk efficiency is the binding wall now.**
It is *not* a per-cell vs position BSP artifact (that bug applies to a
deprecated pipeline). It is the ordinary SAE-budget vs LP-degrees-of-
freedom gap, magnified by hawk's low base rates. Headroom is large —
2.5× current performance would still only reach 50 % efficiency.

## 6. Future phases

The Phase 2B sweep gate ("SAE F1-lift ≥ 50 % of LP F1-lift") failed,
which decides the next direction: **stop adding architectural breadth
at the current SAE budget; introduce capacity and/or supervision.**

### Phase 2C — Closing the SAE/LP wall (concept-targeted SAEs)

Full design note: `docs/diary/2026-05-19_concept-targeted-saes.md`.
Five candidate approaches, ordered by implementation cost. Each is a
separable experiment with a pre-registered decision gate.

| Step | Approach | What it tests | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Wider expansion** at fixed k (exp ∈ {16, 32, 64}) on the F04 / E05 recipes | "Not enough features" vs "wrong features". Baseline for how much pure scale buys before introducing supervision. | Trivial — ~3 h on 3× A6000. |
| 2 | **Higher k** specifically on hawk-targeted runs (k ∈ {128, 192, 256}) | Sparsity-allocation explanation. If k = 256 still doesn't move hawk lift, the objective itself needs supervision, not just bigger budgets. | Same as (1). |
| 3 | **Anchored / guided SAEs** — auxiliary loss `L_anchor = Σ BCE(h_anchor[k], BSP_label_k)` on a designated subset of features (one per hawk BSP). | Whether the SAE *can* represent the threat axis cleanly given gradient pressure to do so. Re-enabled on champTa because completability is now decodable (LP F1-lift = 0.523 fc1 / 0.732 conv2). | Moderate — ~2 days of code. |
| 4 | **End-to-end (E2E) SAEs** — train SAE to preserve downstream Q-head loss (Braun et al., 2024) rather than activation MSE. | Whether threat features are recovered *because* the model uses them. Direct diagnostic for whether champTa's strong play routes through interpretable concepts. | Higher — ~1 week of code + tuning. |
| 5 | **Matryoshka SAEs** — nested dictionaries `d ⊂ 2d ⊂ 4d ⊂ 8d` (Bussmann et al., 2024). Attacks feature absorption / splitting. | Marginal gain on non-threat dense coverage; complement to (3) / (4), not substitute. | Moderate-to-high. |

**Pre-registered decision gates:**

- **Abandon concept-targeting** if step 1 already closes the conv2/hawk
  gap to > 50 % LP efficiency — means we were under-resourced, not
  mis-objectived.
- **Promote concept-targeting to primary direction** if step 2 shows no
  k-driven improvement on conv2/hawk *and* step 3 anchored runs reach
  > 60 % LP efficiency on at least one hawk subcategory.
- **Open a new sub-investigation** if step 4 (E2E) shows the model uses
  concepts the LP misses, or vice versa — would be a new mechanistic
  finding worth its own writeup.

### Phase 2D — Cross-champion concept transfer (conditional)

Triggered only after Phase 2C produces a working concept-targeted SAE
on at least one champion / hook. Train the same anchored SAE recipe on
both champS4 and champTa, compare the resulting feature directories:
strengthens H9 (concept distillation) if champTa's anchored features
are cleaner / closer to LP, or exposes the limits of distillation if
they are not.

### Phase 3 — Generalisation to other games

Tic-tac-toe and Othello are planned but not implemented. The SAE
library (`lib/sae/`) is already **game-agnostic** — game knowledge enters
only via `bsp_labels`, `bsp_schema`, and the per-game module
(`scripts/games/<game>.py`). Bringing a new game online is mostly a
matter of:
1. implementing the game-agnostic activation collection on its trained
   model,
2. defining a BSP schema for that game,
3. running the same LP-then-SAE pipeline.

Othello is the more interesting target because of the existing
literature (Nanda et al. "Othello-GPT") — provides external comparison
points.

### Retired / deprioritized

- **Phase 4** (auxiliary threat-prediction head during DQN training) —
  retired 2026-05-19. Was motivated by champAa's collapsed fc1 threat
  signal; the unified-aux and minimax-distillation training procedures
  solved the bottleneck problem at training time without inference-time
  changes.
- **Broad unsupervised arch sweeps on fc1** (champAa) — settled by
  Anakin sweep (σ = 0.004; second-order effect). Re-enabled for the
  unified-aux family because fc1 there carries threat information.
- **Anchored / matryoshka SAEs on champAa** — would have failed by
  construction (the model doesn't compute completability). Re-enabled
  on champTa.

## 7. Relation to Karvonen et al. (chess / Othello SAEs)

> [INFERENTIAL — written from memory of Karvonen et al. 2024,
> "Measuring Progress in Dictionary Learning for Language Model
> Interpretability with Board Game Models" (arXiv 2408.00113), and
> the closely related Othello-GPT lineage (Nanda 2023, Hazineh et al.
> 2023). Worth double-checking specific numbers against the paper
> before citing externally.]

Karvonen's framework — train SAEs on a board-game model, define a
battery of board-state functions ("BSPs"), match SAE features to those
functions by max-F1 — is **methodologically very close to ours**. The
similarities and divergences both matter for positioning our
contribution:

### What carries over directly

| Their idea | Our analogue |
|---|---|
| Per-square piece-presence / piece-type functions (e.g. "is white knight at e4") | `cell_occupancy` (16) + `cell_attribute` (64) — same shape, just over a 4×4 grid with 4 binary attribute axes instead of {piece-type, color}. |
| Game-state singletons (`in_check`, `can_castle`, `white_to_move`) | `global` (`winning_move_exists`) + `game_phase` (3-way binary). |
| Max-F1 per concept across SAE features as the coverage metric | Identical. We additionally report MCC and F1-lift (§1.1). |
| Linear-probe ceiling as the "what's recoverable" reference | Identical; we use the same `(LP_trained − LP_random)` denominator for the SAE-recovery-efficiency fraction. |
| Random / shuffled feature controls | Identical; we always pair trained-net SAEs with a random-net twin. |

### What we add or do differently

1. **Dual concept basis (gorilla ↔ hawk).** Karvonen's BSPs are atomic
   ("is X attacking Y?"). We carry two parallel bases for the *same*
   threat concept — raw conjunction (`threat_line`: "3-of-4 match
   *and* 1 cell empty") and Nanda-style reframings
   (`reframed_count`, `reframed_completable`, `reframed_any_threat`).
   This was load-bearing: gorilla threat F1 was 0.022 on champAa, hawk
   `reframed_count` F1 was 0.315 on identical activations — 14× more
   signal in the alternative basis. Without the dual basis we would
   have concluded "no threat information present" when the truth was
   "threat information present in a basis our first probe couldn't
   see". This is a methodological lesson we propose generalising:
   board-game interpretability evaluations should report multiple
   bases per concept by default.

2. **F1-lift as headline.** Karvonen reports raw F1 with a precision
   floor to suppress degenerate "always-on" features. F1-lift handles
   the same artifact analytically — `F1 − 2p/(1+p)` collapses the
   trivial-baseline detector to zero without a hand-tuned precision
   knob, and (empirically) is ~3.5× more discriminating in the
   trained-vs-random gap. Cheap to compute, strictly more informative.

3. **Behaviorally grounded competence audit.** Their evaluation
   pre-supposes that the LLM-based game model "knows" the relevant
   concepts because it predicts legal moves above chance. We
   explicitly test that the *Quarto agent uses the concept* via a
   five-test behavior battery (winning-placement accuracy,
   losing-piece avoidance, offered-piece sensitivity,
   illegal-move-Q penalty, late-game entropy). This matters because
   in our project champAa scored 0.405 on losing-piece avoidance —
   close to random — which correctly predicted that defensive
   threat concepts would be near-absent from its fc1 activations.
   We can claim "the model doesn't compute this concept" rather than
   "no SAE recovered it", which is a stronger statement.

4. **CNN DQN vs transformer LM substrate.** Karvonen's targets are
   ChessGPT and OthelloGPT (transformers trained as language models
   over move sequences). We work on a convolutional Q-network trained
   via DQN with replay. The architectural prior is much stronger in
   our setting — random conv filters score LP F1 = 0.78 on cell
   attributes because piece attributes enter as one-hot inputs and
   survive any fixed nonlinear projection (the "random features /
   extreme learning machine" regime). This forced us to introduce
   the **`(cov_SAE − cov_rand_SAE) / (LP_trained − LP_random)`**
   *learned-gap fraction* as a stricter "this is learned-specific,
   not architectural" metric than coverage alone. We expect a port
   of Karvonen's evaluation to CNN game models would need a similar
   correction.

5. **Training-time intervention as the lever.** Their result space is
   "given a fixed pretrained model, how good can SAEs get?". Our
   Phase 2B finding is that **changing the training objective**
   (vanilla DQN → unified-aux trunk → minimax-oracle distillation on
   the SELECT head) reshapes which concepts the model encodes and
   where they live in the network. champAa loses threats at the fc1
   bottleneck (H8 confirmed); champS4 preserves them through fc1
   (H8 overturned); champTa amplifies them (H9 confirmed). This is
   adjacent to but distinct from Karvonen's framing, and is the
   contribution most likely to generalise to non-game neural
   networks.

### What we don't yet do that they do

- **Multi-token / sequential analysis.** Their LLM substrate exposes
  features that change across moves in a single game; our snapshot
  analysis on deduplicated positions cannot see that. If we port to
  Othello / Tic-tac-toe in Phase 3 we should preserve play-order
  information in at least a subset of positions.
- **Causal interventions.** Karvonen uses activation patching to test
  whether SAE features causally drive model behavior. We have not yet
  patched. Worth doing on the strongest champTa SAEs as a follow-up
  to the concept-targeted SAE work (Phase 2C).

### Bottom line

Their framework is essentially the right one for the problem; our
contribution is **(a)** the dual-basis evaluation protocol (gorilla

ladder (Aa → S4 → Ta) that lets us attribute *what changes in the
representation* to *what changes in the loss*. The Karvonen-style
concept battery and SAE-vs-LP ceiling diagnostic transfer directly.
